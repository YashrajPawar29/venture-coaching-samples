# Facilitation Guide: Customer Validation Workshop

**Format:** 90-minute live virtual workshop · 8–10 teams of 3–5 fellows · 1 coach
**Unit:** Customer Validation (after teams have done their first round of interviews)
**Goal:** Each team leaves knowing which of their assumptions are backed by evidence, which aren't, and which interviews to run next.

---

## Before the session (coach prep, ~45 min)

- [ ] Review each team's **Canvas checkpoint**: how many interviews they ran, and their raw notes.
- [ ] Flag teams that are **stuck or under-prepared** (fewer than 3 interviews, only friends and family, or notes made of opinions only). Message them **before** the session with one specific nudge, for example: *"Can you book 2 interviews with people outside your network before Thursday?"*
- [ ] Look at **knowledge-check results** to see which concept the cohort missed most. Use it to set the focus of the concept review below.
- [ ] Optional: run each team's notes through the [Customer Discovery Copilot](https://github.com/YashrajPawar29/customer-discovery-copilot) to see quickly how much of each team's notes are evidence.

## Agenda

| Time | Block | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Welcome and check-in | Attendance. One-word check-in in the chat: *"How did your interviews feel?"* |
| 0:05–0:20 | **Concept review: Evidence vs. Opinion** | Short interactive review (see below) |
| 0:20–0:25 | Set up breakouts | Instructions on screen; teams go to their breakout rooms |
| 0:25–1:05 | **Breakout: Evidence Sort and Assumption Map** | Teams work while the coach rotates through rooms (~4 min per team) |
| 1:05–1:20 | Share-out | 3 teams each share one assumption that was *contradicted* |
| 1:20–1:30 | Commit and close | Each team posts its next 3 interviews and riskiest assumption; survey link |

## Concept review (15 min)

**Live poll:** "Which of these is evidence that the customer has the problem?"
1. "That's a great idea!"
2. "I'd definitely use that."
3. "Last month I spent 6 hours checking job boards and sent 14 emails."
4. "I think a lot of students would want this."

Only **#3** counts. Talk through why:
- **Compliments** (#1) are polite noise.
- **Predictions** (#2, #4) are notoriously unreliable. People are bad at forecasting their own behaviour.
- **Past behaviour** (#3), meaning time, money or workarounds already spent, is the strongest signal.

**AI moment (3 min):** Show an AI-generated "customer persona". Ask: *"Which parts of this did a real customer tell you?"* The point: AI output is a **hypothesis** to test, not a finding.

## Breakout activity (40 min)

1. **Evidence Sort (15 min).** Each fellow takes one interview and highlights each statement as 🟢 behaviour, 🟡 opinion/prediction, or 🔴 compliment.
2. **Assumption Map (15 min).** List the team's assumptions. Plot them on two axes: *How important is it?* × *How much evidence do we have?* Circle the **important + little evidence** corner. That's the riskiest assumption.
3. **Next test (10 min).** Write the cheapest test of the riskiest assumption you could run this week.

## Coach rotation: questions I ask in each room

I spend about 4 minutes in each room. I observe first, then ask **one or two** of these:

- "What did this person actually *do* the last time the problem happened?"
- "You have three 'I'd use it' quotes. Did anyone commit time, money or a referral?"
- "Who did you talk to who *doesn't* have this problem? What does that tell you?"
- "Which assumption, if wrong, would end this venture?"
- "Is this finding from an interview, or from ChatGPT?"
- "What would make you change your mind?"

**What I avoid:** suggesting a solution, picking the team's customer for them, or answering my own question.

## Signs a team is stuck, and what I do

| Sign | What I do |
|---|---|
| Only interviewed friends | Help them write a sourcing plan: 2 communities where their real customer spends time |
| Notes are all opinions | Rewrite one interview question together, from "Would you…?" to "Tell me about the last time…" |
| Team is attached to its solution | Ask them to argue the strongest case that the idea is wrong |
| One fellow is doing all the work | Assign roles for the next round: interviewer, note-taker, synthesizer |

## After the session

- [ ] Check attendance and that everyone completed the survey.
- [ ] Post short written feedback on each team's assumption map within 48 hours.
- [ ] Follow up 1:1 with teams flagged as stuck. Bring in program staff if a team is missing expectations two sessions in a row.
- [ ] Share patterns from the session at the weekly Community of Practice.
