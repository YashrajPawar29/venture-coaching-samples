# Customer Discovery: Interview Guide, Synthesis Notes and Assumption Map

**Fictional venture:** *CampusGigs*, a tool to help college students find on-campus jobs that are still open.
**Team hypothesis:** *"College students waste hours finding on-campus jobs because listings are out of date."*

---

## 1. Interview guide

Based on *The Mom Test*: ask about the customer's life and past behaviour, never about our idea.

**Opening (2 min)**
- "We're students researching how people find campus jobs. There are no right answers, and we're not selling anything."

**Core questions (15 min)**
1. "Tell me about the last time you looked for a job on campus."
2. "Walk me through what you did, step by step."
3. "What was the hardest part? Why?"
4. "How much time did that take? What did it cost you?"
5. "What have you tried to make it easier?" *(listen for workarounds: spreadsheets, Discord groups, emailing professors)*
6. "How did you eventually find your current job?"

**Probing follow-ups**
- "Can you give me a specific example?"
- "What happened next?"
- "Why was that a problem for you?"

**Closing (3 min)**
- "Who else should I talk to about this?" *(a referral is a small but real commitment)*
- "Can I follow up if we test something?"

**Questions we removed, and why**

| ❌ Don't ask | Why | ✅ Ask instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Wouldn't you love an app that shows only open jobs?" | Leading; pitches our solution | "Tell me about the last time a listing was already filled." |
| "Would you pay $5/month?" | Hypothetical; people over-predict | "What have you spent, in time or money, on this so far?" |
| "What do you think of our idea?" | Asks for an opinion; invites compliments | "How do you solve this today?" |

---

## 2. Synthesis notes (4 interviews: 3 students, 1 hiring staff member)

**Evidence (what people did):**
- Student A spent ~6 hrs/week for a month checking boards; sent 14 cold emails and got 2 replies.
- Student C applied to 9 listings; **5 were already filled**. Built a spreadsheet and joined a Discord group to cope.
- Staff member D got **40 emails** for a role filled in week one; forgets to take posts down; left most emails unanswered.
- Student B found their job through a friend and has used the board only once.

**Opinion and compliments (don't count these as validation):**
- "I'd probably use it." (A) · "Maybe, it depends." (B) · "That's cool, good luck!" (C)

**Patterns:**
- **Out-of-date listings** came up in 3 of 4 interviews, from **both sides** (students *and* staff).
- People already use **workarounds** (spreadsheets, Discord, cold emails), a strong sign of real pain.
- Not every student feels the pain: B is satisfied. **The urgent segment is students who need income now.**

**Gaps in our evidence:**
- Only 1 staff interview. Staff are the ones who'd have to close listings. We know little about their motivation.
- No one has committed anything yet (time, money, referrals).

## 3. Assumption map

| Assumption | Importance | Evidence | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Students hit filled or out-of-date listings often | High | Strong (A, C, D) | ✅ Supported |
| The pain is worst for students who need income urgently | High | Medium (A, C vs B) | 🟡 Partly supported |
| Staff will update listings if it takes one click | **High** | **None** | ⚠️ **Riskiest: untested** |
| Universities will pay for this | High | None | ⚠️ Untested |
| Students prefer one source over Discord groups | Medium | Weak | 🟡 Untested |

**Riskiest assumption:** *Hiring staff will mark jobs as filled if it's quick.* If this is wrong, the listings stay out of date and the product fails.

**Cheapest next test (this week):** Ask 5 staff members who post jobs to use a shared form for 2 weeks with a one-click "filled" button. **Measure** what percentage of filled jobs get marked within 48 hours. **Success:** at least 60%.

**Next interviews:** 5 more hiring staff (labs, libraries, dining), and 3 students who found jobs *without* the board.
