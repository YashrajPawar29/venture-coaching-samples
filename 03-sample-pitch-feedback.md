# Written Feedback on a Team's Pitch

**Deliverable:** Final Pitch Package (draft) · **Fictional team:** *StudyBuddy AI*
**Feedback style:** scored against an angel-investor rubric, written as questions and next steps so the team keeps ownership of the venture.

> **Pitch excerpt (fictional):** "StudyBuddy is a revolutionary AI-powered app that will disrupt education. Everyone struggles with studying, and students would love an app that makes it easier. ChatGPT told us the edtech market is worth $400 billion. If we just capture 1% we will be a huge company. There is no competition because nobody else uses AI like we do. We will make money later with ads."

## Feedback to the team

**What's working:** You clearly care about students' learning, and you're excited to use AI. That energy will carry a pitch once the evidence is behind it.

**1. Narrow the problem to one person you can name.**
"Everyone struggles with studying" gives a judge nothing to hold on to. *Who* struggles most, and with *what*? For example: first-year engineering students before their first calculus midterm?
→ **Next step:** Interview 5 students in your chosen segment about the **last time** they prepared for an exam. What did they actually do, and what went wrong?

**2. Replace predictions with evidence.**
"Students would love an app" is a prediction. Judges will ask what customers have already *done*.
→ **Question for you:** What workarounds do students use today (group chats, YouTube, tutors, ChatGPT itself)? Workarounds that already exist are your best proof of a real problem.

**3. Treat AI-generated numbers as a starting point to check.**
A market figure from ChatGPT isn't evidence, and "1% of a huge market" is a classic red flag for investors.
→ **Next step:** Build a bottom-up estimate: *(number of students in your first segment) × (what one would realistically pay)*. Show your working.

**4. Name the alternatives honestly.**
Every venture has competition, even if it's "doing nothing" or "asking ChatGPT directly." Saying "no competition" tells judges you haven't looked.
→ **Question for you:** Why would a student choose StudyBuddy over the free ChatGPT they already use?

**5. Decide who pays.**
"Ads, later" is a placeholder. Will students, parents, or universities pay?
→ **Next step:** Ask 3 of your interviewees what they currently spend on studying (tutors, apps, books).
